# Perrin Family Genealogy.

This record is copied from the book published in 1885. Data was collected by Glover Perrin, Asst. Surgeon General, U. S. Army stationed at the time at Fort Snelling, Minnesota. At considerable cost, with great patience and with the aid of Noah Perrin of Wellesly Hills Mass and Parley Ide Perrin of Taunton Mass. he gathered the information contained in this book. Tombstones, Church records, Town records, War records, Family bible and the Genealogical Records at Boston, with the memory of those living at the time were used to establish a rather complete family tree of the Perrins of America. They helped to build new towns, branched out to the frontier, tilled the soil, many were ministers of the gospel. They fought in every war in which the U.S. ever has engaged in. They played around Boston when it was only five years old. They undoubtedly had a part in the "Boston Tea Party". They were a prolific family and to-day are scattered from East to West. It appears from a book entitled "Emigration to America, from 1600 to 1700, Boston Genealogical Library" that John Perryn came to this country from London England in the ship "Safety" John Grant, Master, landing in the month of August, 1635 at Braintree, Mass. a small town south of Boston, in the Boston Harbor. There he married and resided untill he, with the Rev. Samuel Newman, moved inland, westward a few miles and founded the town of "Rehoboth". His name, variously spelled appear in the towns records. He was buried there. Beside him is his wife "Ann", who is supposed to be one of several Anns who

Fourth Generation

John Perrin, only child of John and Sarah was born March 1692. Died Feb 28, 1731, at Rehoboth. He married Rachel Ide in 1716. She was born in 1695 and died Dec 4, 1780. She is buried beside J. They had six children.

Fifth Generation.

Jesse, fifth child of John and Rachel, was born Jan 24, 1726 Died Jan 15, 1801. He married Rachel Ide, daughter of Daniel Ide, May 11th 1749. She was born Dec 30, 1730 Died Aug 15th, 1808. They had twelve children.

Jesse was town clerk of Rehoboth, Mass. from 1762 till 1780 Rachel is from that Mass. fam of Ides whose descendent, came William Brown Ide, came to California, in its earliest day. He became the first Governor of California.

Rachel Rice Coshow, fifth child of Julia and Robert Coshow, was born near Indianapolis, Ind., Oct 6, 1835. Died May 3 1922. She was married to James Pursell in Blue Grass Co, Iowa Nov 23, 1851, by Rev. Hicks. James was born May 26, 1831. Died Jan 3rd 1915.

James, when years of age joined the Fifth Regiment, under Colonel Jim Blaine and marched to war against Mexico. In 1852 with his family he came to Calif. during the gold rush. They later made two trips to Iowa, In 1859 by ox team bringing back a drove of cattle, and in 1866, again by horse drawn vehicles. He took a load of Chinamen, who had just completed the new through Rail way to Sacramento, to another job in Idaho. He gathered a load of returning

Thomas and Turpenne with their four children went to Oregon in 1878 going by wagon and two mules. 1877 had been a very dry year in the San Joaquin valley. They had reports that Oregon had plenty of rain. So they with James Purcell and family, tacked the popular slogan of the year "Oregon or Bust" on their wagon and took the trail northward. At San Francisco they took the boat, horses, wagon, luggage and themselves, landing at Portland. They staid a year and often recounted that it rain every day. Mules were not adapted to so wet a climate. They bogged down too easily. In the fall of 1879 they returned to Hanford, all the way by wagon, for they had been to Oregon, and were busted too.
