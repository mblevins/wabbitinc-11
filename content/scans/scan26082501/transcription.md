# Gamlin Records

Our great grandfather was born a Gamelin, in Normandy France (as was his wife in the same state). I do not know their given names They came to Canada prior to 1819. and settled in the Province of Quebec.

Our grandfather, Thomas J. Gamelin was born in Canada in 1819. and married Matilde Du Plaze, a French Canadian in about 1845. They had four children. Israel, Born Oct 12 1846; Thomas; born July 21 1849. The civil war came along. Grandpa 43 and son Israel 17 enrolled the same day July 13 1863, at Morristown Vermont, and mustered into service Oct 7, 1863. at Brattleboro Vermont, as privates in the 1st Vermont Heavy artillery. They fought side by side in the Battle of the Wilderness were honorably discharged Aug. 25, 1865. at Washington D.C. Both are listed as born in Canada, and as farmer They were listed as Gamlins, other document listed Gamel

Israel came to Kingston California in 1868, and on up into Sierra Nevada mountains to work in lumber mills. Thomas came in 1869, on the first through continental train from New York to Sacramento. From there he came by stage coach to Kingston to join Israel. Finding his brother up in the mountain he helped build the Spangler house and helped the Geisler in their Kingston Hotel until a lumber freight wagon came out from the Wagy mill. On the return trip he was given a ride up to join his brother. They both spoke English well, for schools and the war had taken all the French accent out of their English speech.

In 1872 the brothers went to work in the Thomas mill.

Now about Grandpa Gamlin and his second family.

Our Grandma was converted at a revival, after they came to Vermont as I remember the story she was baptized in an icy river, when it was necessary to break the ice before dunking them converts A Catholic priest appeared and waded in to wrest grandma from the preacher, hoping to prevent the baptism. Grandpa waded in to help to oust the priest, Quite a water scene took place, but grandma was baptized. The Gamlin boys witnessed the scene, and became strong anti Catholics. Our church had a lovely Easter sunrise service Trumpet call at an out door cross, followed by "Christ arose" by four trumpeters, with the audience singing one verse. It was raining so we went indoors, where I had decorated for just such an emergency. A lovely white cross set in a sea of wild flowers. A sweet little angel in a snow white robe and silver wings sat near. The children put on an Easter service for we oldersters. The preacher gave a sermon. Several hymnal numbers by the trumpet quartette was most sacred. The rain let up and we ate the best breakfast ever. Hot cakes, ham (baked sliced and reheated in an electric roaster.) eggs. both boiled and scrambled, jelly jam, butter, buns, coffee and donuts, on four large tables
